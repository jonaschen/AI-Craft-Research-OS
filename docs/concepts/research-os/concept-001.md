## Motivation

What if an individual researcher could direct an AI-assisted research team while preserving human judgment, explicit evidence standards, and reproducibility?

AI assistance raises questions about how research conclusions can remain inspectable and independently verifiable. This project explores those questions without assuming that adding agents improves research quality.

## Concept definition

AI Craft Research OS is an exploratory research direction: a human-directed, model-independent, evidence-driven workflow in which AI models and agents assist with research, implementation, verification, and review. Humans retain responsibility for research direction, evidence standards, architecture decisions, and final conclusions.

The focus is disciplined coordination of humans, AI capabilities, evidence, and reproducible research artifacts. The concept extends beyond conversational assistance or connecting multiple agents; it does not prescribe an implementation.

## Three principles

- **Model-independent:** Research methods and evidence should not depend exclusively on one AI model provider. Comparisons should document relevant model and environment differences.
- **Evidence-driven:** Claims should be supported by inspectable artifacts, explicit evaluation methods, and reproducible experiments whenever possible. Limitations and negative results should be recorded.
- **Human-directed:** Humans define the questions and boundaries, assess evidence, and make final decisions. AI assistance does not transfer responsibility for conclusions.

## Research questions

1. How can AI-assisted research produce independently verifiable evidence?
2. Which tasks benefit from specialization, and which do not?
3. How can results remain comparable across models and frameworks?
4. How should human review and final judgment be integrated?
5. How can research artifacts be made reproducible and traceable?

These are open questions, not solved problems.

## Non-claims

- No global originality or priority claim is made.
- The architecture and evaluation methodology remain research topics.
